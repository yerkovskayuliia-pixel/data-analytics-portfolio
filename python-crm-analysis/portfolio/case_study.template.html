Most sales were lost before the first conversation

I analysed a year of CRM data from an online IT school in Germany: 20,000 leads, 96,000 sales calls and the full ad budget. The goal was to find the cheapest way to grow profit and design a test that proves it in two weeks.

The data

Four messy CRM tables, turned into one model

The export had the usual problems of a CRM filled in by hand. Language levels were typed in two alphabets ("б1", "в1", "B1 (until February)"). Money came as text in three formats. The CSV export had rounded the 19-digit IDs, so deals no longer matched their contacts. I switched to the Excel export and wrote a reusable cleaning module with 15 rules, each tied to the client's FAQ.

The CRM also had no field for money actually received. I rebuilt it from the first payment, course price, course length and months already studied: €3.58M received out of €6.33M contracted.

Finding 1

The problem is reaching people, not price

Only 4 of every 100 leads paid. The top reasons for losing a deal were "doesn't answer" and "stopped answering", together 40% of lost deals. "Too expensive" was only 4%.

At the same time lead volume tripled during the year, while conversion fell from about 6% to about 3%. The sales team grew its call volume but could not keep up.

Finding 2

Answering in 15 minutes changes the outcome

When a manager responded within 15 minutes, the school reached the client in 77% of cases, against 52% for slower responses. Those leads were also 1.8× more likely to be rated A or B quality (χ² test, p < 0.001).

This is a correlation in historical data, so it needs an experiment before anyone changes the sales process.

Finding 3

Marketing is cheap, so conversion is the lever

All three courses are profitable after marketing. Improving conversion, average payment or number of payments by 10% each adds about €358k margin. Cutting lead cost by 10% adds only €15k.

Two budget fixes stood out too: one Performance Max campaign took 23% of the budget for a single student, and Google Ads leads cost twice as much as Facebook leads at the same conversion.

Hypothesis and test

A two-week experiment the sales team can run

If every new lead is assigned to a manager within one minute, gets an automatic WhatsApp or SMS message, and receives a call within 15 minutes, then the share of leads reached within 24 hours will rise by at least 10 percentage points.

Primary metricLeads with a conversation of 30+ seconds in the first 24 hours. Payment takes a median 17 days, too slow for a two-week test.
Groups50/50 by lead ID. Control keeps the current process.
Sample384 leads per group needed (α = 0.05, power 80%). At 74 leads a day, 14 days give about 520 per group.
GuardrailsShare of non-target leads, calls per manager, opt-outs from messages.
DecisionTwo-proportion z-test with a sample-ratio check. Paid conversion confirmed on the same cohorts after 45 days.
Expected valueAbout +11% conversion and +€385k margin a year if the effect holds.
