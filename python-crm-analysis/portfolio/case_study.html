Data analysis case study · Python

Most sales were lost before the first conversation

I analysed a year of CRM data from an online IT school in Germany: 20,000 leads, 96,000 sales calls and the full ad budget. The goal was to find where sales are lost, pick a growth point in the metric tree, rank five hypotheses for it and design a test that proves the first one in two weeks.

The data

Four messy CRM tables, turned into one model

The export had the usual problems of a CRM filled in by hand. Language levels were typed in two alphabets ("б1", "в1", "B1 (until February)"). Money came as text in three formats. The CSV export had rounded the 19-digit IDs, so deals no longer matched their contacts. I switched to the Excel export and wrote a reusable cleaning module with 15 rules, each tied to the client's FAQ.

The CRM also had no field for money actually received. I rebuilt it from the first payment, course price, course length and months already studied: €3.58M received out of €6.33M contracted. Under German law only the months already studied count as revenue, €3.34M; the other €244k is prepayment, and the share paid by students who dropped out has to be refunded.

Finding 1

The problem is reaching people, not price

Only 4 of every 100 leads paid. The top reasons for losing a deal were "doesn't answer" and "stopped answering", together 40% of lost deals. "Too expensive" was only 4%.

At the same time lead volume tripled during the year, while conversion fell from about 6% to about 3%. The sales team grew its call volume but could not keep up.

Finding 2

Answering in 15 minutes changes the outcome

When a manager responded within 15 minutes, the school reached the client in 77% of cases, against 59% for responses between 15 minutes and 24 hours. Those leads were also 1.8× more likely to be rated A or B quality (χ² test, p < 0.001).

This is a correlation in historical data, so it needs an experiment before anyone changes the sales process.

Finding 3

Marketing is cheap, so conversion is the lever

All three courses are profitable after marketing. Improving conversion, average payment or number of payments by 10% each adds about €334k margin. Cutting lead cost by 10% adds only €15k.

Two budget fixes stood out too: one Performance Max campaign took 23% of the budget for a single student, and Google Ads leads cost twice as much as Facebook leads at the same conversion.

Metric tree

From margin down to the funnel step a manager can change

The tree follows the unit-economics formulas: margin CM = UA × (LTV − LTC) = (CLTV − CLTC) × B, revenue per lead LTV = C1 × CLTV, revenue per student CLTV = (AOV − COGS) × APC. The growth point is conversion C1. It is the product of five funnel steps, so raising any step by some percentage raises C1 by the same percentage.

The teacher's calculation counts people (18,548 contacts, C1 = 4.47%); I count requests (19,822, C1 = 4.22%), because the A/B test randomises requests. Margin and the effect of +10% conversion come out the same either way.

Five hypotheses

One hypothesis per funnel step, ranked by ICE

Each effect is estimated from the data with conservative assumptions. The biggest one, a call script that books the consultation in the first call, needs about 87 days of A/B testing because only 50 leads a day reach that step. Only response speed can be tested within two weeks, so it goes first.

Chosen hypothesis H1 and test

A two-week experiment the sales team can run

If every new lead is assigned to a manager within one minute, gets an automatic WhatsApp or SMS message, and receives a call within 15 minutes, then the share of leads reached within 24 hours will rise by at least 10 percentage points.

Primary metricLeads with a conversation of 30+ seconds in the first 24 hours. Payment takes a median 17 days, too slow for a two-week test.
Groups50/50 by lead ID. Control keeps the current process.
SampleDuration fixed at 14 days. The 15-minute rule runs daily 8:00–23:00 (85% of leads arrive then): 442 leads per group, minimum detectable effect +9.4 pp (baseline 42%, α = 0.05, power 80%), below the expected +12.2 pp estimated from the data. In office hours only, the test would fail (MDE +13.6 pp vs +11.0 pp). Valid only if at least 80% of test leads are called within 15 minutes. Design only: the CRM has no experiment data.
GuardrailsShare of non-target leads, calls per manager, opt-outs from messages.
DecisionTwo-proportion z-test with a sample-ratio check. Paid conversion confirmed on the same cohorts after 45 days.
Expected value+4.5–11% conversion and +€150–375k contribution margin a year if the effect holds.
