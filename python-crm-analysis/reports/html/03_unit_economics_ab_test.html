1. Unit economics by product¶

Unit: a lead (UA). Period: the whole dataset (07.2023–06.2024).

Metric Formula Meaning
UA number of leads User Acquisition
B students Buyers
C1 B / UA conversion to buyer
T number of payments (instalments: 1 payment per month studied; one-off: 1) Transactions
Revenue recognised revenue: course price / duration × months studied
AOV Revenue / T average payment
APC T / B average payments per student
AC ad budget Acquisition Cost
CPA AC / UA cost per lead
CAC AC / B cost per student
ARPU Revenue / UA = C1 · AOV · APC revenue per lead
CLTV AOV · APC revenue per student
CM Revenue − AC contribution margin (before teaching costs)
ROMI CM / AC return on marketing investment

Assumptions:

  1. 82% of leads have no product (they never reached a consultation). So leads and ad budget are allocated to products in proportion to each product's share among leads where it is known. The alternative, counting UA only for leads with a product, would inflate C1 five-fold and understate CPA.
  2. Teaching costs (teacher salaries, platform) are not in the data → CM is the contribution margin after marketing. It is an upper bound of the real margin.
  3. Revenue is what was recognised by the export date, only for months studied (section 1.1): many students are still studying, so Revenue and APC are understated for recent cohorts.

def add_ratios(t: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    t = t.copy()
    t["C1"] = t["B"] / t["UA"]
    t["AOV"] = t["Revenue"] / t["T"]
    t["APC"] = t["T"] / t["B"]
    t["CPA"] = t["AC"] / t["UA"]
    t["CAC"] = t["AC"] / t["B"]
    t["ARPU"] = t["Revenue"] / t["UA"]
    t["CLTV"] = t["AOV"] * t["APC"]
    t["CM"] = t["Revenue"] - t["AC"]
    t["CM_per_UA"] = t["CM"] / t["UA"]
    t["ROMI"] = t["CM"] / t["AC"]
    return t

Reading the unit economics:

  • All three products are profitable after marketing: CAC (≈ €180) is 14–26 times lower than CLTV. Marketing does not limit the business right now; conversion does: out of 100 leads, ~4 buy.
  • Digital Marketing is the main profit source: the highest CLTV and the most students.
  • Web Developer has half the CLTV (the 6-month course is cheaper) at the same cost per lead.
  • APC ≈ 5 payments: students are on average halfway through the course, so revenue will keep growing as they study.

1.1 Revenue recognition: cash received ≠ revenue earned¶

Under German law (a tuition contract, § 611 BGB; the realisation principle, § 252 HGB) the school earns revenue only for the months a student has already studied. A prepayment for future months is a liability (Rechnungsabgrenzung, deferred revenue), not profit: if the student drops out, it has to be refunded.

  • cash_received: money received by the export date (first payment + monthly payments);
  • revenue: recognised revenue = course price / duration × months studied (used everywhere in the project);
  • prepaid = cash_received − revenue > 0: an advance for future months;
  • refund_due: the advance of students who dropped out, to be refunded.

The CRM does not record a student's status, so it is estimated: finished = studied the whole course; dropped out = fewer months studied than have passed since payment, with a 1-month margin; 314 students have no payment date, so their status is unknown.

2. Growth levers: sensitivity analysis¶

We raise each metric by 10% and see how CM changes. When UA grows, the ad budget grows proportionally (CPA is constant); raising C1, AOV or APC needs no extra budget.

def cm(UA, C1, AOV, APC, CPA):
    return UA * C1 * AOV * APC - UA * CPA

Choosing the growth lever. C1, AOV and APC give the same CM uplift (+€334k, +10.5% for every +10%), traffic growth gives less (+€319k, because the budget grows with it), and cutting CPA by 10% adds only +€15k (marketing is already cheap relative to revenue: CAC €179 vs CLTV €3,996). Of the three equal levers we choose C1, because:

  1. C1 has the most headroom: 96% of leads are lost, and 40% of lost deals are "doesn't answer / stopped answering";
  2. AOV and APC are the price and length of study; changing them is risky (price affects demand, and APC grows on its own as students keep studying);
  3. the EDA showed a concrete mechanism for improving C1: speed of first contact.

The 16–20 pp gap holds during working hours and within each major source, so it is not just night-time leads or the channel mix. It is still observational data, which is why an A/B test is needed.

Hypothesis (H1). If every new lead is automatically assigned to an available manager within 1 minute with a push notification, the client gets a WhatsApp/SMS message ("A manager will call you within 15 minutes"), and the manager must call within 15 minutes during working hours, then the share of leads with a conversation in the first 24 hours will grow by at least 10 pp (from ~59% to ≥ 69%), because the client is still "warm" and expects the call. In the data, leads answered within 15 min are reached 77% of the time vs 59% with a response between 15 minutes and 24 hours (an 18 pp gap, 16 pp during working hours; we conservatively assume 10 pp).

  • Product metric we influence: C1 (through reach and qualification).
  • Primary test metric: reached_24h, the share of leads with a conversation of ≥ 30 seconds within 24 hours. Chosen because (a) it is measurable within 1 day, while payment comes after 17+ days on average and cannot be collected within 2 weeks; (b) it is directly linked to C1 (see the metric tree).
  • Secondary metrics: median SLA, A+B lead share, share of Lost with the reason "doesn't answer".
  • Delayed metric: C1 of the test cohorts, tracked 45 days after the end.
  • Guardrail metrics (must not get worse): share of Non target leads, calls per manager per day (workload), share of complaints/unsubscribes from the messages.
  • H0: the reached_24h shares in groups A and B are equal.

5. A/B test design: the duration is fixed at 14 days¶

The test was not run: the CRM export has no experiment data, so below is the design only. The task limits a test to 2 weeks, so we start from the duration: fix 14 days → count the leads that arrive → compute the MDE (the smallest effect the test can detect at α = 0.05 and 80% power). The test makes sense only if the expected effect is at least the MDE.

In [10]:
recent = deals[deals["created_time"] >= "2024-03-01"]
n_days = recent["created_time"].dt.normalize().nunique()
leads_per_day = len(recent) / n_days
alpha, power, TEST_DAYS = 0.05, 0.80, 14


def mde_for(p_base: float, n_group: int) -> float:
    """MDE as a share for two groups of n_group (z-test for proportions, inverse of Cohen's h)."""
    h = NormalIndPower().solve_power(nobs1=n_group, alpha=alpha, power=power, ratio=1)
    return np.sin(np.arcsin(np.sqrt(p_base)) + h / 2) ** 2 - p_base


# control: leads answered between 15 min and 24 h (the "> 24 h" bucket is excluded, see section 4)
p_control = deals.loc[slow, "reached_24h"].mean()
n_14 = int(leads_per_day * TEST_DAYS / 2)
mde = mde_for(p_control, n_14)
expected = 0.10

design = pd.Series({
    "Test duration": f"{TEST_DAYS} days (2 full weeks, covers weekly seasonality)",
    "Leads per day (since March 2024)": f"{leads_per_day:.0f}",
    "Leads per group in 14 days (50/50)": f"{n_14:,}",
    "Baseline reached_24h (control)": f"{p_control:.1%}",
    "α / power": f"{alpha} / {power:.0%}",
    "MDE in 14 days": f"+{mde * 100:.1f} pp (from {p_control:.1%} to {p_control + mde:.1%})",

    "Verdict": "expected effect ≥ MDE: a 14-day test makes sense" if expected >= mde
               else "expected effect < MDE: a 14-day test will not show it",
}, name="value")

Test conditions:

Parameter Decision
Randomisation unit lead (new request)
Split 50/50 by the last digit of the deal ID (even → B, odd → A): deterministic and checkable
Group A (control) the current lead assignment process
Group B (test) auto-assignment + push + automatic message to the client + the "call within 15 min" rule
Who takes part all new leads from all sources; the same managers work with both groups
Duration exactly 14 days, starting on a Monday; no early stopping (the peeking problem)
Validity check SRM test (chi-square) on group sizes; comparison of the source mix
Statistical test two-sided z-test for proportions, α = 0.05; result = difference in shares with a 95% confidence interval
Decision roll out if reached_24h grew significantly and guardrails did not get worse; confirm with C1 after 45 days

Risks: managers know about the test and may also call the control group faster (spillover) → the effect will be underestimated, i.e. the result is conservative. To reduce this, the "≤ 15 min" rule and notifications are shown to managers only on group B leads.

6. Hypotheses for each product¶

The product appears in the CRM only at the consultation, so the unit for product hypotheses is a lead with a known product. The 14-day metric is the share of leads that reached payment or an invoice (Payment Done or Waiting For Payment): payment itself cannot be awaited within 2 weeks (median cycle 17 days), while the client's decision is visible right after the consultation.

Web Developer If a client who received an invoice gets payment reminders after 24 and 72 h with an instalment option, more invoices will be paid, because deals currently stall at the payment stage 14.3% of leads with the product are stuck in Waiting For Payment, more than any other product paid or invoiced → share of invoices paid +5–10 pp (assumption) +43 pp

Verdict on the product hypotheses. Each product has only 3–4 leads with a known product a day, i.e. 19–30 leads per group in 14 days. With this traffic a 2-week test can only detect an effect of +35–43 pp, while a realistic effect is 5–10 pp. Even with all three products pooled, the MDE stays around +23 pp. Therefore:

  1. A product-level A/B test within 14 days is not possible: there is not enough data, and the result would be "no difference" whether or not the change works.
  2. Product hypotheses should be checked as a pilot with a before/after comparison or once traffic grows; the A/B test within 14 days runs on the all-lead metric (section 5), where the MDE of +8.4 pp is below the expected effect.
  3. The website page (page) does not help: product pages bring only 1.6–3.2 leads a day. Product tests become possible only when traffic grows or the product is recorded for every lead at sign-up.

8. Product analytics summary¶

  1. All products are profitable after marketing; CAC €175–189 vs CLTV €2.5–4.5k (ROMI 13–25×). The bottleneck is conversion (C1 ≈ 4%).
  2. The growth lever is C1: the most headroom and a clear improvement mechanism (40% of lost deals are "doesn't answer / stopped answering").
  3. Metric tree: CM ← Revenue ← C1 ← reach ← first-contact SLA.
  4. Hypothesis: auto-assigning leads + contact within 15 minutes will raise 24-hour reach by ≥ 10 pp.
  5. A/B test (design only, no experiment data): the duration is fixed at 14 days, 519 leads per group, MDE = +8.4 pp vs an expected +10 pp, α = 0.05, power 80%, z-test for proportions; final check on C1 after 45 days.
  6. Product hypotheses: Digital Marketing — evening group, UX/UI — alumni case and portfolio review after the consultation, Web Developer — reminders and instalments for issued invoices. With 3–4 leads with a product a day the 14-day MDE is +35–43 pp, so product A/B tests within 2 weeks are not possible; they are checked as pilots.
